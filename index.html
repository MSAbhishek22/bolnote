        The problem

        The big note-taking apps were not built for the recordings already sitting in your phone. On Otter's own pricing page (read in November 2026): the free plan stops a conversation at 30 minutes, allows 3 imported files in your whole lifetime, and transcribes in six languages — Hindi is not one of them. If your lecture, interview or client call is in Hindi or Hinglish, the tool simply cannot hear it.

        What BolNote does instead

        Upload-first, nothing else. No bot joining your calls, no account, no minute pool. Your recording is cut into short pieces, transcribed by Whisper (which does speak Hindi), and stitched back into one transcript. The ridge above is your recording's own shape — tap it, or any line of text, to hear that exact moment. Notes are made only from your transcript, and every point carries the second it came from.

        Honest limits

        • No speaker names. The transcription service used here does not label who said what. Lines are split by pauses, not by person.
        • Free quota. Transcription runs on a free tier, so very heavy use can hit a daily ceiling. Nothing is ever charged.
        • Your audio leaves your device once — to the transcription API, in pieces — and is not stored by BolNote. The notes are made from the transcript text only.
        • Mixed language is hard. Hinglish code-switching is exactly what this is built for, and also where mistakes will happen. The timestamps are there so you can verify any line in one tap.
